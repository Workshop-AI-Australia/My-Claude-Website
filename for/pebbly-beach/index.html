  1. Red flag 01 / 06Answered

    New to your platform

    The concern

    “He’s never used Kelpie.”

    My answer

    True. My stack has included n8n, Make, Zapier and Claude, not Kelpie. I learn platforms by working through real problems. I’d learn your platform on the job, not claim I already know it. I’d map onboarding, workspace configuration and adoption to your stack.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask me to map a familiar workflow onto Kelpie.
    • Check where I ask for help rather than guess.
  2. Red flag 02 / 06Answered

    A consultant, not your employee

    The concern

    “Will he own the engagement, or just advise?”

    My answer

    I’m already the day-to-day client lead: understanding the work, agreeing what matters and staying accountable for the engagement. This is an application, not a consultancy proposal. I’d bring that ownership and my full attention to this role. Advice alone isn’t the result.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask how I handle a blocked engagement.
    • Trace a decision from client need to delivery and follow-through.
  3. Red flag 03 / 06Answered

    Smaller businesses, familiar work

    The concern

    “His clients are closer to 10 to 30 people than 400.”

    My answer

    They are. I won’t pretend those sizes are the same. My work with Mattick Air, a construction client and a logistics client overlaps your trades and industrial focus. The work is familiar; the larger scale is something I’d need to learn.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask what changes at 400 people.
    • Check how I’d handle data ownership, cleanup and competing priorities.
  4. Red flag 04 / 06Answered

    Training that survives the session

    The concern

    “Will people use it when he’s gone?”

    My answer

    At Mattick Air, when group training didn’t stick, I switched to one-to-ones using people’s own work. At Ailo, an enablement platform I built had 83% monthly use across 19 users. My test is whether people can work without me in the room.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask what changed after the Mattick Air sessions.
    • Check what the Ailo usage figure measures, not just the percentage.
  5. Red flag 05 / 06Answered

    Built is not the same as running

    The concern

    “Has he shipped workflows, or only shown demos?”

    My answer

    A construction client’s nightly AP invoice-check agent runs in production. Their SharePoint index agent is built and in handover. Mattick Air’s email-to-SimPro workflow is shipped. A logistics client’s pipeline has no AI at run time. I’d separate what’s running, what’s handed over and what’s still being built.

    How you’d check it
    • Walk through each workflow and its actual status.
    • Ask about exceptions, human checks and who owns the next step.
  6. Red flag 06 / 06Answered

    Value, not an AI activity report

    The concern

    “Will he track value, or just count things built?”

    My answer

    I’d set the programme rhythm around value: regular updates, decisions and an owner for each outcome. ROI comes before the build. My four questions test whether to proceed. I follow Australia’s Guidance for AI Adoption: named owner, risk first, and someone who can switch it off.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask me to work through the four questions on one problem.
    • Check the baseline, intended return and stop decision.
