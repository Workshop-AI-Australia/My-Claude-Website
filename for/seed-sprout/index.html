Built for day three thousand.

Your ten-year letter says good design is how something works on day three thousand, and that the next decade is about systems rather than swaps. The same is true inside a business. Here's what that could look like for a team of 18, put together from public information only.

Prepared byJustin Watts
DateOctober 2026

What we don't know. Everything here comes from your website, your reviews and what you've shared in the press. We don't know how your team works day to day, so treat every example as a guess to correct, and tell us where we're wrong.

The idea runs in three steps, each feeding the next. The team learns to use AI in their own jobs. That shows us what's worth building. The builds show us which of your tools need connecting.

01 · LearnEveryone gets better at their own job.

Sessions by role, online and recorded, so they fit a team that works flexibly. Everyone leaves with one thing they'll use the next morning, and we check in a fortnight later on what stuck.

Customer care

Faster replies in your voice

AI drafts the reply from your policies and the order history. A person reads it and decides what's sent.

Product and design

Every comment, read in minutes

Before a redesign, see everything customers have said about a product across reviews, messages and socials.

Marketing and content

One video, five uses

Turn one of Sophie's videos into a blog post, a help article, an email and a set of captions, all in the brand's voice.

Warehouse and supply chain

What's in someone's head, written down

Turn the way the best packer does it into a checklist anyone can follow during the Christmas rush.

Wholesale

Stockist questions without the digging

Draft answers on stock, pricing and lead times from your own price list and stock levels.

Finance and leadership

Month-end in an afternoon

Summaries, board papers and what-if numbers drafted from your reports, ready for you to check.

03 · ConnectYour tools, talking to each other.

Most of the answers your team needs already exist, spread across different tools. Connected, anyone can ask a question in plain English and get an answer drawn from all of them. It starts read-only, and you set the rules on what it can do.

Tools we can see from outside · you'd tell us the rest

We've done this before. For Mattick Air, a Melbourne commercial air-conditioning company, we connected their job management system so the office can ask it questions in plain English, built an email agent for their helpdesk, and set up their monthly client reports to build straight from live data.
