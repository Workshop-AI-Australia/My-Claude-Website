  1. Red flag 01Answered

    No community at network scale

    The concern

    “He’s never run a WhatsApp community across 63 partners.”

    My answer

    I’ve never moderated an online community. Auto Enthused, my TikTok channel, is an audience of 3.2M+ views, not a community. I facilitate in-person workshops of about 12 people, and chaired a local volunteer governing body’s meetings of nine. I’d start with power users and help them lead.

    How you’d check it
    • Agree measures up front: engagement depth, and who moves from literacy to building.
    • Count the circles members lead themselves.
  2. Red flag 02Answered

    No frontier AI partnerships

    The concern

    “He has no partnerships with frontier AI companies.”

    My answer

    Not yet. I take no software resale or referral fees, so my advice on AI tools stays independent. At Everyone Eats I built a program delivered by a partner, Hunger Free World. From 2012 to 2018 I sat on the Asia Pacific advisory council for the Public Interest Registry (.org).

    How you’d check it
    • Ask how I set up the Hunger Free World partnership.
    • Any AI partner I suggest earns me no fee.
  3. Red flag 03Answered

    Not from education

    The concern

    “He’s from small business and property tech, not education.”

    My answer

    Mostly, yes. But I founded Everyone Eats, a social enterprise in Melbourne and Bangladesh, and I raised about $25,000 to fund 96,000 meals. I hold a current Working with Children Check. At Ailo I trained non-technical colleagues to build their own tools.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask to see my current Working with Children Check.
    • Ask what my Ailo colleagues built after the training.
  4. Red flag 04Answered

    Training that creates dependence

    The concern

    “Will he be a trainer who makes people dependent on him?”

    My answer

    Fair question. My test is whether people can build without me in the room. At Mattick Air, when a group session didn’t change how most people worked, I switched to one-to-ones on their own work. At Ailo, an enablement platform I built had 83% monthly use across 19 users.

    How you’d check it
    • A few months on, count who still uses what they learnt, and who has built something of their own.
  5. Red flag 05Answered

    Hype over responsible adoption

    The concern

    “Will he hype AI to educators, or help them adopt it responsibly?”

    My answer

    I’m not an AI evangelist. I follow Australia’s Guidance for AI Adoption: a named owner for each system, risk assessed first, and a person who can switch it off. A logistics client’s pipeline has no AI at run time, because it didn’t need it.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask me to talk privacy and responsible use with a group of educators.
    • Watch whether people feel safe to say they’re unsure.
  6. Red flag 06Answered

    Global work, and Spanish

    The concern

    “Our network is global, across many time zones, and Spanish is a plus.”

    My answer

    I’ve lived or worked in five countries: Australia, the UK, the US, France and Bangladesh. The .org council served a global non-profit membership. I speak B2 (intermediate) French. I don’t speak Spanish, and I won’t claim to.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask about the .org council’s global membership.
    • Test my French if it helps. For Spanish, the answer today is no.
