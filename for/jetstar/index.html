  1. Red flag 01 / 06Answered

    No day-to-day governance register

    The concern

    “He’s never run a governance framework and register day to day.”

    My answer

    I haven’t run a governance framework or register day to day. I do use four questions before any build, and a clear no on the first three means no build. Named owner, risk first, and someone who can switch it off.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask me to walk the four questions on one use case.
    • Check who owns it and who can stop it.
  2. Red flag 02 / 06Answered

    ISO and NIST are new to me

    The concern

    “He hasn’t worked to ISO/IEC 42001 or the NIST AI RMF.”

    My answer

    I haven’t worked to ISO/IEC 42001 or the NIST AI RMF. I follow the Australian Government’s Guidance for AI Adoption with clients: named owner, risk assessed first, and a clear stop authority. I won’t claim either framework or any certification.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask which guidance I actually use with clients.
    • Ask me to map one Jetstar use case to ISO/IEC 42001 or NIST AI RMF.
  3. Red flag 03 / 06Answered

    Agentic controls at enterprise scale

    The concern

    “Has he run agentic AI controls and governance gates?”

    My answer

    Not at enterprise scale. Mattick Air keeps a person on the SimPro trigger. A construction client’s SharePoint agent won’t answer from an index more than 48 hours old. Each build I hand over lists what it can access, how to stop it, and how its access keys get renewed.

    How you’d check it
    • Walk a shipped agent: trigger, stop and key renewal.
    • Ask what happens when the index is stale.
  4. Red flag 04 / 06Answered

    A new consultancy

    The concern

    “Workshop AI is new. Will that hold?”

    My answer

    Workshop AI started in 2026, after nearly twelve years at Ailo. The consultancy is new; the work isn’t. At Ailo I led teams, owned a software budget and ran major platform moves. I’d bring that operating experience, not a long client list.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask what I ran at Ailo before founding Workshop AI.
    • Ask one of my referees how I operated.
  5. Red flag 05 / 06Answered

    No airline experience

    The concern

    “He’s never worked in an airline.”

    My answer

    True. I’ve never worked in an airline. At Ailo and with each Workshop AI client, I started by sitting with the people doing the work and learning the operation before building anything. I’d do the same at Jetstar: learn the operation before I build.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask what I learnt when I first sat with a new client.
    • Ask one of my referees how I learn unfamiliar work.
  6. Red flag 06 / 06Answered

    Will people actually use it?

    The concern

    “Will people actually use what he puts in place?”

    My answer

    Judging value, feasibility and data readiness is core. At Mattick Air I switched to one-to-ones when group training didn’t stick. At Ailo, an enablement platform I built had 83% monthly use across 19 users. I’d build AI Champions until people don’t need me in the room.

    How you’d check it
    • Ask what the Ailo 83% figure actually measures.
    • Check what changed after the Mattick one-to-ones.
  7. Closing noteNot a flag

    Almost a fit, with clear gaps

    The note

    A note on fit, not another red flag.

    In my words

    I’m almost a good fit but I miss out on these particular areas. I’m sure you’ll find someone who has 100% of what you’re after. If it does turn out you’ve got space for a purely enablement, training and upskilling role, please let me know.

    What I’m asking
    • Tell me if there’s room for a purely enablement, training and upskilling role.
