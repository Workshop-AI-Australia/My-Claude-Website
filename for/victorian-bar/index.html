    1. Red flag 01 of 06NOTED

      “He hasn’t worked in legal practice.”

      That’s right, and I won’t dress it up. I spent nearly twelve years at Ailo, then started Workshop AI this year, advising owners in construction, logistics and trades. I’d start with a handful of barristers and one piece of work they want off their desk.

      How to check itMy capability statement says it plainly, under Relevant experience: “I haven’t worked in legal practice.”

    2. Red flag 02 of 06NOTED

      “A consultancy isn’t the usual shape for this role.”

      It isn’t, and I wouldn’t be a usual hire. I’d work with the Bar through Workshop AI, as a consultant. I’d do this as a Workshop AI engagement. The work lines up closely with mine, and the person you’d deal with is me.

      How to check itSection 03 of my capability statement, “A Workshop AI engagement”.

    3. Red flag 03 of 06NOTED

      “His clients are small businesses. The Bar represents more than 2,300 barristers.”

      They are, and a program at the Bar’s scale would be new to me. The way I’d start is one piece of work, then spread what works. At Ailo I owned a software budget of about $150k a year, and every engagement starts with the return assessed.

      How to check itAsk me what I’d pick as the first piece of work, and how I’d know it was spreading.

    4. Red flag 04 of 06NOTED

      “This role coordinates cyber, privacy and vendor assessments. He isn’t a security assessor.”

      I’m not, and knowing that is part of the work. Each system I work on has a named owner, and a person who can switch it off. When a question needs a specialist, I’d bring in Barristers’ Chambers Limited and your advisers rather than guess.

      How to check itAsk me which questions I’d hand to a specialist, and which I’d answer myself.

    5. Red flag 05 of 06NOTED

      “Workshop AI only started this year.”

      It did. The years behind it aren’t new: nearly twelve were at Ailo, a property software company, where I taught non-technical colleagues to build their own tools and led teams of up to ten. Only the practice name is new.

      How to check itSection 02 of my capability statement, under Relevant experience and Collaboration and accountability.

    6. Red flag 06 of 06NOTED

      “He hasn’t assessed a legal technology product.”

      Assessing a legal technology product would be new to me. I’m vendor neutral: I don’t resell software or take referral fees. For a logistics business, the right design was plain automation with no AI at run time, and it’s live.

      How to check itAsk me what I’d check first when a legal technology vendor gives a demonstration.

    7. Bonus note · 07

      “What would he do first?”

      I start with what a wrong answer would cost. For the Bar, that means privilege, confidentiality and the integrity of legal work, turned into rules a barrister can follow. Then I’d find one piece of work a barrister wants off their desk.

      How to check itPage 2 of my capability statement. The fourth of my four questions is “What does a wrong answer cost?”
