Every trade business owner knows the van at the end of the day. The job is done, the tools are packed, and the notes are a problem for later.

Later is where the trouble starts.

By the time the notes get written, the details have gone soft. Which part was swapped. What the customer asked about on the way out. Whether the second unit needs a follow-up visit. The office has to chase the tech, the tech has to remember, and the invoice waits for both of them.

The question is not how the notes get written. It is when. Let's dig in.

Firstly, notes written on site are written while the job is still in front of you. You can look at the unit, the part number, the meter. There is nothing to remember because nothing has been forgotten yet.

And the advantage won't stop there. The office gets what it needs without a phone call. The quote for the follow-up can go out while the customer still remembers who you are. Nobody has to decode a voice message recorded on the highway.

![Diagram of three steps in order: finish the job, write the notes on site, the office has what it needs](assets/img-02.png)

This is the kind of job where talking beats typing. A tech can talk through the job before getting in the van, then tidy it into the format the office already uses. The writing part, the part everyone hates, gets a lot smaller.

None of this needs new software to start. It needs the notes to happen before the engine starts.

Write the notes before you leave the site.
