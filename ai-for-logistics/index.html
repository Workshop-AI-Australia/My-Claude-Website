AI for logistics and transport businesses

In transport and logistics, AI is strongest on the paperwork and messages around the freight: booking requests from email, proof of delivery checks against invoices, surcharges that never get billed, customer updates, and the records you need for Chain of Responsibility. Routing and dispatch decisions are better left to dedicated software and your people.

Where the time goesFive office jobs AI handles well in logistics

  • Bookings from email. Booking requests arrive as emails, spreadsheets and PDFs. AI reads them, sets up the booking in your transport management system for a person to confirm, and asks the customer for anything missing.
  • Proof of delivery against invoices. AI checks that every delivered job has a POD attached and that the invoice matches what was delivered, then lists the exceptions for a person to chase.
  • Surcharges. Waiting time, detention, tail lifts, futile pick-ups and redeliveries are common freight surcharges, and they're easy to miss. AI can read driver notes and POD comments and flag jobs where a surcharge should have been billed.
  • Customer updates. Same-day changes, delays and failed deliveries mean a stream of messages. AI drafts them from the job status in your tone, and a person sends or approves.
  • Reporting. Late and failed deliveries by customer, lane and reason, written up in plain English each week rather than assembled by hand.

Chain of ResponsibilityRecords AI can keep in order

The Heavy Vehicle National Law applies in Victoria, and the amended law started on 1 August 2026, including updated fatigue rules and simpler work diary record-keeping. Under Chain of Responsibility, parties such as employers, operators, schedulers, consignors, loaders and unloaders each have a primary duty to ensure the safety of their transport activities so far as is reasonably practicable. That duty can't be handed to anyone else, including software.

What AI can do is keep the records behind it complete. Fatigue records, such as work diaries for work 100 kilometres or more from base and local records such as daily run sheets, must be kept for three years, and the NHVR expects all work records to be truthful, factual and complete. AI can check run sheets and records for gaps and inconsistencies every day and list them for a person to resolve.

For dangerous goods, the ADG Code applies in Victoria under regulations administered by WorkSafe Victoria. AI can check transport documents against your checklist before the load leaves. It doesn't replace your dangerous goods training or your responsibility.

A sensible first projectFind the unbilled surcharges

Run AI across the last three months of completed jobs, PODs and driver notes, and list every job where waiting time, a tail lift, a futile pick-up or a redelivery was recorded but not invoiced. Then run the same check every week.

It uses data you already have, and the answer is a dollar figure, which makes it a fair test of whether AI deserves more of your time.

5 questionsQuestions we hear

Can AI plan our routes?

That's usually a job for dedicated routing and dispatch software, which is built for it. AI is more useful around it: reading bookings in, updating customers, checking PODs and reporting on what went wrong.

Can AI read booking emails into our transport management system?

If your system has an API or an import, usually yes. AI reads the email or attachment, builds the booking and a person confirms it. Start with one customer's bookings, measure the accuracy, then widen it.

Can AI help with Chain of Responsibility?

It can help with the records, not the duty. AI can check fatigue records and run sheets for gaps, keep documents complete and flag inconsistencies, but the primary duty stays with each party in the chain and can't be delegated.

Can AI find surcharges we forgot to bill?

Yes, and it's a good first project. AI compares driver notes and POD comments with invoices and lists jobs where waiting time, tail lifts, futile pick-ups or redeliveries were recorded but not charged.
